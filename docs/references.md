# Entmotif — Annotated Bibliography

Sources behind the design reasoning, with **what each one actually tells us** and
how it bears on entmotif. **Citation keys** (e.g. `[SchellenbergHabashi2015]`) are
used in `docs/bit-budget.md`, `this.i`, and the paper, so a claim can always be
traced to its source. Each entry records a **verification status**: *verified* =
the cited claim was checked against the source (abstract/summary) on the date
noted; *secondary* = cited within a verified source or by the 2026-06-23 review
but not independently fetched — **cite with care; never present as verified.**

## Core empirical sources (verified 2026-06-23)

### `[SchellenbergHabashi2015]` — the empirical backbone
Schellenberg, E. G., & Habashi, P. (2015). *Remembering the melody and timbre,
forgetting the key and tempo.* Memory & Cognition, 43(7), 1021–1031.
<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25802029/>
- **What it found:** Unfamiliar (novel) melodies consolidate after just **two
  exposures**, with **no forgetting over a week**, in **unselected listeners**.
  Memory for **timbre is durable at all delays (10 min / 1 day / 1 week),
  "perhaps indefinitely"**; memory for **key and tempo fades** (gone by 1 week).
- **Bears on entmotif:** The backbone of Model B's feasibility — recognition of a
  novel motif persists a week *given ≥2 exposures* (our load-bearing exposure
  assumption). Vindicates **timbre/style as the master, most-durable dimension**
  and confirms zeroing **absolute key** and treating **tempo** as weak.
- **Verification:** verified 2026-06-23.

### `[DowlingKwakAndrews1995]` — contour vs. interval over time
Dowling, W. J., Kwak, S.-Y., & Andrews, M. W. (1995). *The time course of
recognition of novel melodies.* Perception & Psychophysics, 57(2), 136–149.
<https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03206500>
- **What it found:** **Contour** discrimination dominates after **short/empty**
  delays; **pitch-interval** discrimination dominates after **long filled**
  delays. Coherent **tonality** aids interval encoding; **rich, real melodies**
  aid contour discrimination.
- **Bears on entmotif:** The **contour** bit is a strong *short-term* hook that
  weakens over long unrehearsed delays relative to interval-in-tonality — a
  time-conditioning, not a deletion; our tonal, "real" motifs partly mitigate.
  Source of the contour `‡` note in `bit-budget.md §4`.
- **Verification:** verified 2026-06-23.

### `[HebertPeretz1997]` — melody > rhythm in long-term memory
Hébert, S., & Peretz, I. (1997). *Recognition of music in long-term memory: Are
melodic and temporal patterns equal partners?* Memory & Cognition, 25(4),
518–533. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9259629/>
- **What it found:** For naming **familiar** tunes, **melody (pitch) is a far
  better retrieval cue than rhythm**; rhythm *alone* is a **poor** cue. The
  *best* cue is the **combination** of pitch + rhythm.
- **Bears on entmotif:** Supports a modest haircut to the **melodic-rhythm-motif**
  row over long delays. **Caveats to honor when citing:** (1) the paradigm is
  *naming familiar tunes* (semantic memory), not discriminating *novel* motifs
  (our Model B) — a domain-transfer assumption; (2) it does **not** touch the
  **groove-bed** rhythm that carries style. Source of the rhythm `†` note.
- **Verification:** verified 2026-06-23.

### `[GjerdingenPerrott2008]` — rapid genre recognition (corrects a review misread)
Gjerdingen, R. O., & Perrott, D. (2008). *Scanning the dial: The rapid
recognition of music genres.* Journal of New Music Research, 37(2), 93–100.
<https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09298210802479268>
- **What it found:** Listeners categorize genre **astonishingly fast** — above
  chance for 10 genres from clips as short as **250 ms**. The **44%** figure is
  the *agreement between 250 ms judgments and the same listeners' 3 s judgments*
  (a speed measure), **not** an accuracy ceiling.
- **Bears on entmotif:** **Refutes** the review's style-downgrade: genre/timbre
  recognition is **fast and robust**, so the ~5-bit **style** row stands.
  Recorded here so the misread is not repeated.
- **Verification:** verified 2026-06-23 (corrects the review's reading).

### `[PeretzVuvan2017]` — amusia prevalence
Peretz, I., & Vuvan, D. T. (2017). *Prevalence of congenital amusia.* European
Journal of Human Genetics, 25, 625–630.
<https://www.nature.com/articles/ejhg201715>
- **What it found:** In **N≈20,000**, congenital amusia prevalence is **~1.5%**
  (not the 1980 single-test 4%); **not attenuated by training**; ~46% of
  first-degree relatives affected (heritable); slightly more females.
- **Bears on entmotif:** Fixes the population figure; amusia degrades the budget
  **unevenly** — kills pitch/contour/mode, spares timbre/rhythm — weakening the
  melodic half, not everything.
- **Verification:** verified 2026-06-23.

### `[Smit2022]` — major/minor affect is enculturated
Smit, E. A., Milne, A. J., Sarvasy, H. S., & Dean, R. T. (2022). *Emotional
responses in Papua New Guinea show negligible evidence for a universal effect of
major versus minor music.* PLOS ONE, 17(6), e0269597.
<https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0269597>
- **What it found:** "Major = happy / minor = sad" is **strongly tied to
  Western-music exposure**; a minimal-exposure PNG community did not show it
  (Bayesian; cannot fully exclude universality). Roughness-based stability *is*
  cross-cultural.
- **Bears on entmotif:** The **mode/cadence affect** bits are
  **Western-enculturated-listener** bits, not universal — an accessibility/scope
  condition, not a deletion for the likely target audience.
- **Verification:** verified 2026-06-23.

## Benchmark & secondary sources (cite with care)

### `[Loss2009Randomart]` — the visual-analog benchmark
Loss, D. *Visualizing keys (the OpenSSH "drunken bishop" / randomart algorithm).*
<http://www.dirk-loss.de/sshvis/drunken_bishop.pdf>
- **Use:** randomart turns a 128–160-bit SSH-key hash into ASCII art with an
  estimated **~20–24 perceptual bits** recoverable by eye — the visual sibling
  benchmark entmotif (and entviz) measure against.
- **Verification:** secondary (benchmark figure inherited from entviz; not
  re-fetched this session).

### Corroborating / cited-not-fetched
- **Dowling (1978)**, *Scale and contour: Two components of a theory of memory
  for melodies* (Psychological Review) — contour as a primary melodic-memory
  component. *Secondary.*
- **Dowling & Bartlett (1981)** — contour dominates at short delays (~75% at
  >5 min); corroborates `[DowlingKwakAndrews1995]`. *Secondary.*
- **Edworthy (1985)**, *Interval and contour in melody processing* — contour vs.
  interval over time; shorter sequences recognized better. *Secondary.*
- **Levitin & Cook (1996)** — listeners retain **absolute tempo** of *familiar*
  recordings; cited for why absolute tempo is durable only for known songs (hence
  zeroed for novel motifs). *Secondary — not independently verified.*
