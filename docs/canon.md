# entmotif Canon — great, earworm-defensible themes (with sources)

Built per `prompts/canon/build-canon.md`. Purpose: a cross-genre list of themes
that are *hard to argue with* as memorable earworms, selected by **cross-source
consensus** (a theme that recurs across many independent reputable rankings is
hard to dispute). We will transcribe each kept theme's identifiable phrase to ABC
(`experiments/teaching/canon/abc/<id>.abc`, phase 2) and grade them with our
faculty to calibrate the rubric (`prompts/teaching/rubric.md`).

## How this list was built (the inputs behind every pick)

Inclusion was driven by **independent, reputable rankings** — critics' lists,
large public polls, broadcaster/industry rankings, data-driven frequency
databases (e.g. jazzstandards.com most-recorded), and — most decisive when
present — **academic earworm/catchiness studies**. The two academic anchors that
recur below:

- **`[Jakubowski2017]`** — Jakubowski, Finkel, Stewart & Müllensiefen (2017),
  *Dissecting an earworm: Melodic features and song popularity predict
  involuntary musical imagery*, Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity & the Arts
  (Goldsmiths; n≈3,000). Names the most-reported earworms (Bad Romance, Can't Get
  You Out of My Head, Don't Stop Believin', Somebody That I Used to Know, Moves
  Like Jagger) and the *features* (fast tempo, common global contour, unusual
  intervals/repetitions) that make a tune stick. APA release:
  <https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2016/11/earworms>.
- **`[HookedOnMusic]`** — Burgoyne et al., "Hooked on Music" (Museum of Science &
  Industry Manchester / University of Amsterdam; ~12,000 players), measuring
  recognition speed. Top results: Wannabe (2.29 s), Eye of the Tiger, SOS, Beat
  It. Coverage: <https://time.com/3554568/wannabe-spice-girls-catchiest-song/>.

A theme **named in an earworm/catchiness study** is maximally defensible and is
tagged `[study]` in its "Why" line.

### Verification status (same discipline as `docs/references.md`)

Each source in the **Sources** section is tagged:

- **verified** — the page was actually fetched and the claim read on 2026-06-23.
- **secondary** — the list/claim was seen only in a web-search snippet (real URL,
  not independently fetched). *Cite with care; do not present as verified.* Many
  rankings below are secondary; they still establish consensus by recurring
  across multiple independent secondary sources, but a single secondary source is
  weak evidence on its own.

Research was done by four parallel web-research passes (classical+film/TV;
games+rock+pop; soul+jazz+country; folk+broadway+world+jingles). Where an agent
could not fetch a page, the source is marked secondary rather than verified.

### Legend

- **`★ core`** = confident core (~25): undisputed, multi-source, the universal
  earworms. **`strong`** = solid top-50. The **long tail** (everything else we
  found) is preserved in its own section below — we keep the tail, not just the 50.
- **Notation-visible greatness?** `yes` = recognizable by tune alone (best fit for
  an instrumental engine) · `partly` = melody recognizable but identity also leans
  on timbre/lyric/production · `mostly-not` = fame is *not* in the melody (flagged;
  may not survive notation — itself a useful finding).
- IDs `C01–C50` are the curated canon; `T01–T60` is the long-tail backlog.

# The canon — top 50

## Classical / orchestral

## C01 — Symphony No. 5 in C minor ("Fate"), opening — Ludwig van Beethoven, 1808, classical · ★ core
- Phrase: The four-note motif — three short Gs and a held E♭ ("da-da-da-DUM"), restated a step lower, driving the first ~4 bars. The single most universal classical incipit.
- Why it qualifies: Classic FM "15 most famous tunes" ("perhaps the four most famous chords of all time"); classical-music.com "15 greatest openings" ranks it #1. Recognizability undisputed.
- Notation-visible greatness?: yes — pure rhythm/pitch motif, no timbre or lyric dependence.
- Transcribe: yes

## C02 — "Ode to Joy" (Symphony No. 9, 4th mvt) — Ludwig van Beethoven, 1824, classical · ★ core
- Phrase: The stepwise, mostly-conjunct chorale tune (~8-bar main theme) that climbs and falls within a narrow range — singable by anyone. The EU anthem.
- Why it qualifies: classical-music.com pop-culture list (Olympics, A Clockwork Orange, EU anthem); recurs as a top recognizable theme across recognizability surveys.
- Notation-visible greatness?: yes — a stepwise melody is its essence; works unaccompanied.
- Transcribe: yes

## C03 — Eine kleine Nachtmusik (Serenade No. 13), 1st mvt — W. A. Mozart, 1787, classical · strong
- Phrase: The opening Allegro's rising arpeggiated G-major "rocket" answered by a descending turn ("tum-ti-tum, ti-tum-ti-tum-ti-tum"), first ~4–8 bars.
- Why it qualifies: Classic FM "15 most famous tunes"; classical-music.com "15 greatest openings" (#8) and pop-culture list (#7) — three independent appearances.
- Notation-visible greatness?: yes — melodic/rhythmic; instantly hummable.
- Transcribe: yes

## C04 — William Tell Overture, finale ("March of the Swiss Soldiers") — Gioachino Rossini, 1829, classical · strong
- Phrase: The galloping 6/8 trumpet fanfare — repeated charging "ta-da-dum, ta-da-dum, ta-da-dum-dum-dum" (the Lone Ranger theme).
- Why it qualifies: Classic FM "15 most famous tunes" ("instantly recognisable for its galloping rhythm"); recurs in the multi-source recognizability survey.
- Notation-visible greatness?: yes — rhythm + trumpet melody carry it; recognizable hummed.
- Transcribe: yes

## C05 — "In the Hall of the Mountain King" (Peer Gynt Suite No. 1) — Edvard Grieg, 1875, classical · strong
- Phrase: The creeping minor-key ostinato — a stepwise, plucked, repeated 8-bar figure that starts slow/quiet then accelerates and crescendos.
- Why it qualifies: Classic FM "15 most famous tunes" ("incredibly catchy main theme"); cited again in the multi-source recognizability survey.
- Notation-visible greatness?: yes — the tune and its accelerando are the hook; not timbre-dependent.
- Transcribe: yes

## C06 — "Ride of the Valkyries" (Die Walküre) — Richard Wagner, 1856, classical · strong
- Phrase: The leaping triplet-based brass call (dotted "da-da-DA-da-da" upward-leaping motif), repeated and harmonized — the ~8-bar Valkyrie theme.
- Why it qualifies: classical-music.com pop-culture list (#2, Apocalypse Now); a top recognizable theme in the classical recognizability survey ("Kill the wabbit").
- Notation-visible greatness?: yes — the leaping brass melody is the hook, hummable alone.
- Transcribe: yes

## Classical / orchestral

## T01 — Also sprach Zarathustra, opening ("Sunrise") — Richard Strauss, 1896, classical
- Phrase: The C–G–C trumpet fanfare over an organ/bass drone, answered by timpani, building to the full-orchestra chord (the "2001" fanfare).
- Why it qualifies: classical-music.com "15 greatest openings" (#6) and pop-culture list (#1, 2001: A Space Odyssey).
- Notation-visible greatness?: partly — the rising trumpet motif is melodic, but impact leans on orchestral timbre and the organ-to-tutti swell.
- Transcribe: yes

## T02 — Toccata and Fugue in D minor, opening — J. S. Bach (attrib.), c. 1704, classical (organ)
- Phrase: The dramatic descending opening flourish — a mordent-topped figure tumbling down, then the held diminished chord.
- Why it qualifies: Classic FM "15 most famous tunes"; classical-music.com "15 greatest openings" (#14) and pop-culture list (#11, Fantasia).
- Notation-visible greatness?: partly — recognizable gesture, but much identity is the pipe-organ timbre.
- Transcribe: yes

## T03 — "O Fortuna" (Carmina Burana) — Carl Orff, 1936, classical (choral)
- Phrase: The thunderous opening — hammered chords and timpani under a unison choral incantation, then the driving ostinato verse.
- Why it qualifies: classical-music.com "15 greatest openings" (#2). (Thinner independent corroboration than the core classical picks; heavy trailer use.)
- Notation-visible greatness?: partly — driving rhythm and choral cell transcribe, but impact is choral mass + percussion timbre.
- Transcribe: yes

# Gaps / notes for the curator

- **South Asia is underrepresented.** No single clear melodic-consensus world
  candidate surfaced for India (e.g. a famous raga theme, "Vande Mataram", or a
  Bollywood hook). Flagged as a gap to fill in a later pass.
- **The Classic FM 2024 film poll is idiosyncratic at the very top** (Hans
  Zimmer's "The Holiday" at #1), so film consensus weighted the juried AFI list
  and the BBC public poll more heavily.
- **Two academic studies (`[Jakubowski2017]`, `[HookedOnMusic]`) name mostly
  pop/rock songs** — they validate the *methodology* (fast tempo, common contour,
  unusual intervals/repetition → stickiness) and directly endorse specific pop
  picks, but no academic earworm study ranking *classical/film/jazz* themes was
  found. Those genres rest on critics'/poll consensus instead.
- **Most non-fetched rankings are tagged secondary below.** Consensus is
  established by recurrence across multiple independent secondary sources; treat a
  lone secondary source as weak.
